
==== Sunday, the 4th of January, 1931 a quarter to ten in the evening ada ====
It was a quarter to ten in the evening on Sunday, the 4th of January, 1931, and the day was frosty and bright. Mrs. Ada Turner was a charwoman of forty-one, a war-widow who lived in two rooms in Bermondsey with her son Bert, a docker. She was honest and chapel-going, proud and sharp-tongued, but kind, and very poor. She had a war-widow's pension of ten shillings a week, which stopped when she married again in 1921; that second husband ran off within the year, and she would not speak of him. Her husband Fred, a carman, was killed at Passchendaele in October 1917; she kept his silver watch on the mantelpiece and his medals in a tin.

She was in her two rooms in Bermondsey, with Bert Turner, and had a pound six and tenpence in her purse. The fire was out; the room was bitterly cold, with frost on the inside of the window. There was food in the cupboard for 2 meals. The coal-box was empty. It was dark but for the fire; there was no penny in the meter. Bert was in work at the docks. She was chilled to the bone.

She remembered: (Friday) Bert gave her his half of the rent and his board, 11s. 6d. (Yesterday) Ada pawned her other silver pencil for 0s. 9d.. (Yesterday) Ada pawned the third silver pencil for 0s. 9d.. Ada was very low in spirits and walked out alone; P.C. Dawe saw her home. Mrs. Nolan came across the landing with a cup of tea and sat with her a long while; Bert was told.

The rent was paid up (since 2 January). She meant to get coal for the fire in this cold.
