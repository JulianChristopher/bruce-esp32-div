
==== Monday, the 5th of January, 1931 four o'clock in the afternoon ada ====
It was four o'clock in the afternoon on Monday, the 5th of January, 1931, and the day was frosty and bright. Mrs. Ada Turner was a charwoman of forty-one, a war-widow who lived in two rooms in Bermondsey with her son Bert, a docker. She was honest and chapel-going, proud and sharp-tongued, but kind, and very poor. She had lived in the same two rooms in Bermondsey for eleven years and had been behind with the rent only twice. Her husband Fred, a carman, was killed at Passchendaele in October 1917; she kept his silver watch on the mantelpiece and his medals in a tin.

She was in her two rooms in Bermondsey, alone, and had a pound ten and fivepence in her purse. The fire was out; the room was bitterly cold, with frost on the inside of the window. There was food in the cupboard for 11 meals. Bert was in work at the docks. She was hungry; She was chilled to the bone.

She remembered: Mr. Thomas has given her notice: she has lost her place cleaning the Moorgate office, twelve shillings a week. Ada pawned Bert's Sunday suit for 5s. 0d.. Ada pawned her late husband's silver watch for 10s. 0d.. Ada was very low in spirits and walked out alone; P.C. Dawe saw her home.…

Mrs. Turner had been given notice at the Moorgate office (since 5 January). Ada owed the tradesman in Bermondsey 2s. 0d. (since 4 January). The rent was paid up (since 2 January). She meant to find new cleaning work to make up the twelve shillings she lost and pay back the tradesman in Bermondsey the 2s. 0d. she borrowed.

==== Thursday, the 8th of January, 1931 ten o'clock in the morning ada ====
It was ten o'clock in the morning on Thursday, the 8th of January, 1931, and the day was bitterly cold, with a hard frost. Mrs. Ada Turner was a charwoman of forty-one, a war-widow who lived in two rooms in Bermondsey with her son Bert, a docker. She was honest and chapel-going, proud and sharp-tongued, but kind, and very poor. Barker had known her for years and had let her had things on the slate before, but he talks. Her husband Fred, a carman, was killed at Passchendaele in October 1917; she kept his silver watch on the mantelpiece and his medals in a tin.

She was in her two rooms in Bermondsey, alone, and had a pound twelve and sixpence in her purse. The fire was out; the room was bitterly cold, with frost on the inside of the window. There was food in the cupboard for 7 meals. The coal-box was empty. Bert was in work at the docks. She was chilled to the bone.

She remembered: (Long ago) "He has always been a good son to her, and has never given her a moment's anxiety." A letter came from her sister Lizzie, at Paddock Wood in Kent: A happy New Year to them both. The hop-gardens will want pickers in September; Bert…

Mrs. Turner had been given notice at the Moorgate office (since 5 January). Ada owed the tradesman in Bermondsey 2s. 0d. (since 4 January). The rent was paid up (since 2 January). She meant to find new cleaning work to make up the twelve shillings she lost, get coal for the fire in this cold and pay back the tradesman in Bermondsey the 2s. 0d. she borrowed.
